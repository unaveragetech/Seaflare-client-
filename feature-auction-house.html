Feature — Auction House Suite

The market, watched continuously.

Listing a lot is a verified sequence, not a hopeful click. Every automation here re-checks real inventory and market state before it moves to the next step.

Sell-lot assembly

Assembles an exact quantity into the hotbar through verified clicks, then lists it — no guessing whether the item actually moved.

Arbitrage scanning

Flags listings priced under recent market history so an underpriced item doesn't sit unnoticed.

Standing order rules

Sell and buy rules that keep retrying on their own schedule and report exactly why an attempt failed.

How it works

Every step confirms itself before the next one fires.

This is the part most automation gets wrong: trusting that a click worked because nothing threw an exception. Here's what actually happens between "list this item" and it showing up on the auction house.

1. Assemble the exact quantity

If the target slot already holds more than needed — a full stack of dirt when you only want to list one — the assembler lifts the whole stack, places back exactly the quantity wanted, and parks the remainder somewhere else in the inventory. If it holds less, the assembler pulls from other matching stacks until the count is exact. Every lift and every placement is followed by a real re-read of the slot: the assembler polls for the expected count to appear (accounting for real network latency to the server) before considering a step done, and retries the same click a bounded number of times if it hasn't.

  • Trim, merge, or split — whichever the current inventory state actually needs.
  • Stray-cursor recovery — clears anything left over from an interrupted prior attempt before starting.

2. List and confirm

Once the lot is confirmed sitting in the sell slot, the client dispatches the listing command and watches for the held item to actually leave the hand — that disappearance is the reliable success signal, since most price points don't open a confirmation screen at all. A green confirmation pane is clicked through when one does appear, but it's never required for the listing to be counted as successful.

3. Track it afterward

Live listings sync periodically opens the auction house, reads back your own listings, and reconciles them against what the client expected to be there — so a sold, expired, or cancelled listing gets picked up automatically rather than sitting stale in memory.
