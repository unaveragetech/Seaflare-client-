Feature — RTP Mapper

Turn a teleport gamble into a search.

Every /rtp becomes a data point. Samples land on a live map, regions rotate evenly, and a run can hunt for a spot matching your own criteria instead of just wandering.

Region rotation

Cycles every enabled region evenly, mixing in plain /rtp at a ratio you control as a control group against targeted sampling.

Cluster detection

Notices when a region keeps re-dropping you on already-mapped ground and rotates away before wasting the whole sample budget.

Stop-rule search

Evaluates every landing against your own criteria and halts the whole run the instant one matches.

How it works

The mapper learns the server's real limits instead of guessing them.

DonutSMP doesn't publish its /rtp cooldown, and it doesn't tell you when a request was silently ignored. The mapper is built around not trusting either of those things.

Cooldown auto-backoff

A fire that produces no teleport looks identical to a fire that's simply still on cooldown — the server gives no chat message either way. Rather than hardcode a guessed delay, each consecutive miss doubles an extra wait stacked on top of the configured floor, and a single landed sample clears the backoff back to zero. Over a run, the effective delay converges on whatever the server is actually enforcing, rather than a fixed number quietly drifting wrong.

  • Halts after repeated misses — a set number of fires with no teleport stops the run rather than burning cycles forever.
  • Late-teleport capture — if a teleport lands just after the client gave up waiting, the sample is still captured in place.
Effective wait per fire (backoff rising, then clearing on a landing)

The overlay survives a teleport

Every single /rtp makes the game swap in the terrain-loading screen, replacing whatever was open — including the map overlay. Nothing in the mapper's own logic ever closes it; the client does that on its own, on every sample. A watcher restores the exact same map instance once the world settles, which is what keeps your pan position, zoom level, and colour mode intact instead of resetting the view every single teleport.

Search, not just survey

Arm a set of criteria — distance range, biome, whatever defines "a good spot" for what you're hunting for — and every fresh landing gets evaluated against them the instant it arrives. A match halts the run immediately and reports the reason, instead of the mapper continuing to sample past the exact spot you were looking for.
